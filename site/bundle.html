Multilingual Message Intelligence

A system that reads short messages in English, Hindi and Hinglish (Hindi typed in English letters) and works out what language each one is in, whether it is spam, how the sender feels, and what people are talking about.

    How it works

    Every message passes through four models, one question each.

    STAGE 1Which language is this?
    How
    Cuts the text into 1 to 4 letter chunks, weights rare chunks higher (TF-IDF), and a classifier picks the language. If it is not confident enough, the message goes to review.
    Learned from
    UN texts in 15 languages, English SMS and tweets, and real Hinglish tweets (L3Cube).
    CodeChef idea
    Language Detection System
    97.5%on real Hinglish tweets
    STAGE 2Is it spam or a scam?
    How
    Words, letter chunks and scam cues (links, phone numbers, money symbols) feed a classifier. The score is a true probability, and each language has its own cut-off so genuine messages are rarely blocked.
    Learned from
    The UCI SMS Spam Collection, with duplicate messages removed.
    CodeChef idea
    Email Spam Classifier
    96.3%spam F1 on English SMS
    STAGE 3Is the sender happy or upset?
    How
    Word and letter patterns feed a linear classifier. One model for English (positive or negative) and one for Hinglish (negative, neutral or positive).
    Learned from
    English tweets and short reviews, and real Hinglish comments (IIIT-H).
    CodeChef idea
    Movie Review Sentiment Analyzer
    69.4%Hinglish macro F1 (3 classes)
    75.9%English macro F1 (tweets)
    STAGE 4What are people talking about?
    How
    Each message becomes a vector of numbers from word embeddings, and K-Means groups similar messages into 6 themes per language. No labels needed.
    Learned from
    Genuine English SMS and real Hinglish comments.
    CodeChef idea
    Clustering and Dimensionality Reduction
    Chosen over TF-IDF with PCA for stability

    The comparison behind stages 1 to 3

    For each of these stages we trained three kinds of model on the same data: Naive Bayes, a linear SVM and KNN. Then we compared them fairly. This is the algorithm comparison from the CodeChef Iris project.

    Smart routing

    Spam stops the line, so a scam is never scored for sentiment. Messages whose language is unclear are held for review instead of guessed.

    Results

    We ran the pipeline on a demo sample of 340 real held-out messages in 14 languages. 142 were Hinglish, and 7% of the English and Hinglish messages were flagged as spam.

    On a larger stream of 3,921 held-out messages, the language stage answered 96.3% of them and was right 99.1% of the time when it answered. The full pipeline processes 63 messages per second on one CPU core.
